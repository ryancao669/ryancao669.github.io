Lesson 1: Personal Banking

A simple overview of the project and the first lesson on managing money.

What this lesson covers

This lesson introduces the basics of personal banking: bank accounts, debit and credit cards, budgeting, and fees and interest.

Bank accounts

A bank account is a safe place to store money. Checking accounts are for everyday spending, while savings accounts are better for money you want to set aside over time.

A simple example is using checking for groceries and bills while keeping an emergency fund in savings.

Debit and credit cards

Debit cards pull money directly from checking. Credit cards let you borrow money and pay it back later, which means they can build credit history but may also charge interest if not paid on time.

The main takeaway is to understand how each one works before using it regularly.

Budgeting

A budget is a plan for how your money comes in and where it goes. It helps you prioritize needs, prepare for emergencies, and save toward bigger goals.

One practical first step is to track your spending for a week and group purchases into categories.

Fees and interest

Fees are charges that can happen when account rules are broken, for example overdraft fees. Interest is money a savings account can earn over time.

Reviewing account terms and understanding avoidable fees is one of the easiest ways to build better money habits.

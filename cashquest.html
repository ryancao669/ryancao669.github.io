Lesson 1

Personal Banking

Subtopic

Bank accounts

What is it A bank account lets you store money. You can deposit checks and use a debit card.

How does it work Checking is for daily spending. Savings is for money you want to set aside.

Example You buy food with checking and keep emergency money in savings.

What students need to take away Knowing account types helps you manage money responsibly.

Action Choose the account type that fits how you spend and save.

Vocabulary Checking account and savings account plus deposit and debit card.

Subtopic

Debit and credit cards

What is it A debit or credit card connects to a bank account or a line of credit.

How does it work Debit pulls money right away. Credit lets you borrow and pay later.

Example You use debit for a snack. You use credit for a bigger buy and pay the bill later.

What students need to take away Debit uses money you have. Credit is borrowed money that needs care.

Action Decide which card fits your habits and your goals.

Subtopic

Budgeting

What is it A budget tracks how much you earn and how much you spend.

How does it work Listing income and costs helps you save and plan ahead.

What students need to take away A budget puts you in control of your money.

Action Track every purchase this week and compare it to your guess.

Subtopic

Fees and interest

What is it Banks can charge fees. Banks can also pay interest on savings.

How does it work Fees can happen after an overdraft or low balance. Interest grows savings.

What students need to take away Fees cost money. Interest earns money over time.

Action Check your account rules and find ways to avoid fees.

Lesson 2

Employment and Taxes

Subtopic

Types of employment

What is it Jobs can be full time or part time or self employed.

How does it work Full time often has steady hours and benefits. Part time has fewer hours. Self employed workers manage their own taxes and benefits.

Takeaway Your job type affects pay, benefits, and taxes.

Action Pick the job type that matches your interests and your lifestyle.

Subtopic

Gross pay vs net pay

What is it Gross pay is what you earn. Net pay is what you take home after deductions.

How does it work Employers take money from each paycheck for state tax and federal tax and FICA.

Takeaway Budget with net pay since it is what you can spend.

Action Use an online tool to estimate take home pay.

Vocabulary FICA is a payroll tax for Social Security and Medicare.

Subtopic

Taxes and how they work

What is it Taxes are required payments that fund public services.

How does it work The U.S. uses progressive tax brackets. Higher income faces higher rates.

Example Part of a $30,000 salary falls into the 12 percent bracket.

Takeaway Knowing brackets helps you plan and avoid surprises.

Action Look up tax brackets and estimate take home pay for a dream job.

Vocabulary Progressive tax means rates rise as income rises.

Subtopic

Filing taxes

What is it A tax return compares what you owe with what was taken out.

How does it work You file by April 15 using a W-2. You get a refund if too much was withheld.

Example Joe had $1800 withheld. He owed $1400. He got a $400 refund.

Takeaway A refund is your own money coming back.

Action If you earned income, file to see if you get money back.

Vocabulary W-2 is a wage and tax statement.

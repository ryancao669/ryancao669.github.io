Lesson 1: Personal Banking

This lesson covers the essentials of personal banking with a clear format.

Topic

Personal Banking

A clean overview of bank accounts and cards plus budgeting and the basics of fees and interest.

Subtopic

Bank accounts

What is it

A bank account is a safe place to store money. You can deposit checks and buy things with a debit card.

How does it work

There are different account types. Checking is for daily spending while savings is for longer term goals.

Example

You put your paycheck in checking for purchases. You keep an emergency fund in savings to grow over time.

What students need to take away

Learning personal banking helps people manage money responsibly.

Action students can take

Open the right account type for your needs. Use checking for daily spending and savings for money you will not spend often.

Vocabulary terms

Checking account and savings account plus deposit and debit card.

Subtopic

Debit and credit cards

What is it

Debit and credit cards are payment tools. They connect to a bank account or a line of credit.

How does it work

A debit card pulls money right away from checking. A credit card lets you borrow money and pay it back later. You may pay interest if you miss payments.

Example

You buy groceries with a debit card and the balance drops. You use a credit card for a laptop and pay by the due date.

What students need to take away

Credit can build history when used responsibly. Late payments can hurt your score and add fees.

Action students can take

Track your spending and plan how you will pay a credit card bill before you use it.

Vocabulary terms

Debit card and credit card plus credit limit and interest and billing cycle.

Subtopic

Budgeting

What is it

A budget is a plan that tracks how much money you earn and how much you spend.

How does it work

You list income and expenses. This helps you prioritize needs and build savings.

Example

You set aside money for rent and groceries first. You automate a weekly transfer into savings.

What students need to take away

Budgeting helps you prepare for emergencies. It also helps you save for bigger goals.

Action students can take

Track your spending for one week. Categorize every purchase.

Vocabulary terms

Income and expenses plus savings and needs and wants.

Subtopic

Fees and interest

What is it

Fees are charges from the bank for certain actions. Interest is money the bank pays you for keeping savings with them.

How does it work

Accounts can charge fees for overdrafts or low balances. Savings accounts can grow through interest over time.

Example

Spending more than your balance triggers an overdraft fee. Keeping $500 in savings earns a small monthly interest deposit.

What students need to take away

Avoid fees by monitoring balances. Look for accounts that reward saving.

Action students can take

Review your bank account terms. List any fees you can avoid.

Vocabulary terms

Overdraft and minimum balance plus interest rate and fee.

My thoughts on eBay buying Depop

When eBay announced it was buying Depop, it sparked a lot of controversy on social media. However, the deeper you dive into what this actually means for how Depop will operate, the more interesting it gets.

Let's start with trades, because this is a serious issue on Depop. As of now, Depop's trade system runs entirely on trusting the other person. When you agree to swap an item with a stranger, one person ships first and then you hope for the best. Many issues occur on both the buyer and seller side as each can ghost each other once they receive the item or make a payment. However, eBay is completely different as they built their trade mechanisms around these kinds of problems with features like Buyer Protection and Authenticity Guaranteed. So the big question is, will any of this apply to any part of Depop?

Upon more research, it seems that Depop will remain as an individual platform for the most part, but some aspects are subject to change. Depop may gain access to eBay's improved shipping, payment, and authenticity checks. If that becomes true, it would truly change how trades work. Items would be sent to a middleman, then verified and shipped similarly to how other reselling companies operate like GOAT for sneakers and luxury clothing. Whether Depop chooses to follow through with this is a different question. A big appeal of Depop is how simple and casual it is. Implementing all these advanced eBay features would scare away users that chose Depop for its simplicity.

Which brings us to the replica (reps) issue, and arguably one of the messiest parts of Depop. Anyone who has spent five minutes on Depop scrolling through items knows that fakes and reps are everywhere on the website. Sellers constantly sell these while claiming them to be authentic when they really aren't. The big question is: what if eBay added an authenticity guaranteed program to Depop to prevent the selling of replica items?

If I was in eBay's position, I would add a separate option to allow buyers to choose whether or not they would like Buyer Protection on their item. This would keep Depop's casual appeal to most teenagers, but wouldn't drive users away as it wouldn't be too complicated. Many Depop users tend to just sell their old clothes, whilst eBay users resell more expensive items for the purpose of profit. If eBay offered more serious options instead of just straight out enforcing them, it would lead to people staying on both platforms, being a win for both sides.
